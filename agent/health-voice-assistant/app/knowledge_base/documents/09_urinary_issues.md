## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Urinary Tract Infection (UTI) — Mild / Lower Tract
**Symptoms:** burning sensation during urination, frequent urge to urinate, cloudy or strong-smelling urine, mild lower abdominal discomfort, feeling of incomplete bladder emptying  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Drink 2–3 liters of water daily to flush bacteria  
2. Drink unsweetened cranberry juice (proanthocyanidins help prevent bacterial adhesion)  
3. Take Paracetamol for pain relief  
4. Use a hot water bottle on the lower abdomen for comfort  
5. Urinate frequently — never hold urine for prolonged periods  
6. Wipe front to back after using the toilet to prevent reinfection  

**When to seek help:** fever, blood in urine, flank/back pain (suggests kidney involvement), symptoms worsen after 48 hours, recurrent UTIs (>3/year), pregnant women with UTI symptoms  

**DO NOT:** use perfumed soaps or bubble baths near genital area, consume excessive caffeine or alcohol, delay urination  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: HIGH
### Condition: Kidney Stones (Renal Calculi)
**Symptoms:** severe colicky pain in the side and back below ribs, pain radiating to lower abdomen and groin, pink/red/brown urine, nausea and vomiting, painful urination, urinating in small amounts  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Drink 2.5–3 liters of water daily to help pass the stone  
2. Take NSAIDs (Ibuprofen 400mg with food) — first-line pain relief for renal colic  
3. Apply heat pad to the flank area for muscle spasm relief  
4. Strain urine through a filter to catch the stone for analysis  
5. Reduce dietary sodium, oxalate-rich foods (spinach, nuts, chocolate), and animal protein  

**When to seek help:** pain so severe you cannot sit still, fever and chills (suggests infection), complete inability to urinate, persistent vomiting preventing fluid intake  

**DO NOT:** ignore blood in urine, reduce fluid intake thinking it reduces pain, self-medicate with antibiotics  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE
