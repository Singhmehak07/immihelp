## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Sprains and Strains
**Symptoms:** pain and tenderness around the joint, swelling and bruising, limited movement, popping sensation at time of injury, difficulty bearing weight (ankle/knee)  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Follow the RICE protocol: Rest, Ice (20 min every 2–3 hours for first 48 hrs), Compression (elastic bandage), Elevation (above heart level)  
2. Take Ibuprofen 400mg with food every 8 hours for pain and swelling  
3. After 48–72 hours, begin gentle range-of-motion exercises  
4. Use an ankle brace or support wrap for added stability  
5. Gradually return to activity — do not rush recovery  

**When to seek help:** unable to bear weight, joint appears deformed, severe swelling immediately after injury, numbness in the affected area, no improvement after 5–7 days of home care  

**DO NOT:** apply heat in the first 48 hours, massage the injured area initially, try to "walk it off" if pain is severe  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Arthritis Pain (Osteoarthritis)
**Symptoms:** joint stiffness especially in the morning or after inactivity, aching pain in joints (knees, hips, hands, spine), swelling around joints, crackling or grating sensation (crepitus), reduced flexibility  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Apply warm compresses or use a warm bath for morning stiffness  
2. Use cold packs after activity if joints are swollen  
3. Take Paracetamol for mild pain or Ibuprofen (with food) for pain with inflammation  
4. Apply topical Diclofenac gel or Capsaicin cream to affected joints  
5. Do low-impact exercise: walking, swimming, cycling, gentle yoga  
6. Maintain a healthy weight to reduce stress on joints  
7. Use assistive devices if needed (walking stick, jar openers)  

**When to seek help:** sudden severe pain, joint is hot and red (may indicate infection or gout), significant reduction in mobility, symptoms not controlled by OTC medication  

**DO NOT:** remain sedentary for long periods, do high-impact activities (running, jumping), ignore progressive joint deformity  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Muscle Cramps
**Symptoms:** sudden severe involuntary muscle contraction, visible hardening of the muscle, sharp pain, usually in calves, thighs, or feet, often occurs at night or during/after exercise  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Gently stretch and massage the cramping muscle  
2. Apply a warm towel or heating pad to the cramped muscle  
3. Drink water or an electrolyte solution (may indicate dehydration)  
4. Take a warm bath with Epsom salts (magnesium sulfate)  
5. Eat potassium-rich foods: bananas, oranges, potatoes  
6. Ensure adequate magnesium intake: nuts, seeds, leafy greens  
7. Stretch calves before bed to prevent nighttime cramps  

**When to seek help:** cramps happen frequently without obvious cause, severe cramps not relieved by stretching, muscle weakness accompanies cramps, leg swelling or skin changes (may indicate vascular issue)  

**DO NOT:** ignore chronic recurring cramps, exercise without proper warm-up, allow dehydration during physical activity  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE
