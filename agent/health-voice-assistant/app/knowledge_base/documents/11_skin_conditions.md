## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)
**Symptoms:** dry, itchy, inflamed skin, red or brownish-grey patches, small raised bumps that may leak fluid when scratched, thickened cracked scaly skin, raw sensitive skin from scratching  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Apply fragrance-free moisturizer (emollient) generously at least twice daily — best applied within 3 minutes of bathing  
2. Use lukewarm (not hot) water for bathing, limit to 10–15 minutes  
3. Apply 1% hydrocortisone cream to affected areas for up to 7 days for flare-ups  
4. Use colloidal oatmeal baths for itch relief  
5. Wear soft, breathable cotton clothing  
6. Keep nails short to minimize damage from scratching  
7. Use a humidifier in dry environments  

**When to seek help:** signs of infection (pus, increased redness, warmth), eczema spreads rapidly, not responding to OTC treatment after 7 days, disrupting sleep significantly  

**DO NOT:** use harsh soaps or detergents, scratch (use cold compress instead), wear wool or synthetic fabrics directly on skin  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Acne (Acne Vulgaris)
**Symptoms:** whiteheads, blackheads, pimples, red tender bumps, large painful lumps beneath the skin (cysts), oily skin  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Wash face gently twice daily with a mild cleanser (avoid scrubbing)  
2. Apply Benzoyl Peroxide 2.5–5% gel to affected areas (start low to reduce irritation)  
3. Use Salicylic Acid 0.5–2% wash or toner to unclog pores  
4. Apply oil-free, non-comedogenic moisturizer  
5. Avoid touching or picking at pimples  
6. Change pillowcases frequently  
7. Use oil-free, non-comedogenic sunscreen  

**When to seek help:** severe cystic acne, acne causing scarring, not improving after 8 weeks of OTC treatment, acne causing significant emotional distress  

**DO NOT:** pop or squeeze pimples (causes scarring and infection), over-wash face, use greasy cosmetics, use multiple harsh products simultaneously  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Fungal Skin Infection (Ringworm / Tinea)
**Symptoms:** circular red scaly patch with clearer center (ring-shaped), itchy, may spread outward, can appear on body, scalp, feet (athlete's foot), or groin (jock itch)  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Apply antifungal cream (Clotrimazole 1% or Miconazole 2%) twice daily for at least 2–4 weeks  
2. Keep the area clean and dry  
3. Wear loose-fitting breathable clothing  
4. For athlete's foot: change socks daily, use antifungal powder in shoes  
5. Wash towels, bedding, and clothing in hot water  
6. Do not share personal items (towels, combs, clothing)  

**When to seek help:** infection on the scalp (may need oral antifungal), not improving after 2 weeks of treatment, spreading despite treatment, signs of secondary bacterial infection  

**DO NOT:** stop antifungal treatment early even if symptoms improve, scratch the area, share personal items  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Boils and Skin Abscesses
**Symptoms:** painful, red, swollen lump under the skin, gradually increases in size, may develop a white or yellow center (head), surrounding warmth and tenderness, may cause fever if severe  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Apply warm compress for 20 minutes, 3–4 times daily to promote drainage  
2. Once the boil drains naturally, clean with antiseptic solution  
3. Cover with a sterile bandage  
4. Take Paracetamol or Ibuprofen for pain  
5. Wash hands thoroughly before and after touching the boil  
6. Wash towels and clothing that contact the boil in hot water  

**When to seek help:** boil larger than 5cm, fever accompanies the boil, boil on the face or spine, multiple boils, red streaks extending from boil, does not drain within 2 weeks, recurrent boils, immunocompromised patient  

**DO NOT:** squeeze or try to pop the boil, share towels or razors, use homemade tools to lance it  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE
