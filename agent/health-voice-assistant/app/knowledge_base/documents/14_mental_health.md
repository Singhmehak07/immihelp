## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Mild Anxiety
**Symptoms:** persistent worry, restlessness, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance, fatigue, racing thoughts, feeling on edge  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Practice deep breathing: inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 6 seconds (repeat 5–10 times)  
2. Do progressive muscle relaxation: tense and release each muscle group from toes to head  
3. Exercise regularly — 30 minutes of moderate activity (walking, jogging, yoga) most days  
4. Limit caffeine and alcohol intake  
5. Maintain a consistent sleep schedule  
6. Practice mindfulness or meditation (even 10 minutes daily helps)  
7. Talk to a trusted friend or family member about your feelings  
8. Write in a journal to externalize worries  

**When to seek help:** anxiety interferes with work/school/relationships, panic attacks, persistent physical symptoms (chest pain, palpitations), thoughts of self-harm, reliance on alcohol or drugs to cope  

**DO NOT:** self-medicate with alcohol or recreational drugs, isolate yourself completely, ignore persistent symptoms hoping they will go away  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: HIGH
### Condition: Mild to Moderate Depression
**Symptoms:** persistent sadness or low mood lasting >2 weeks, loss of interest in previously enjoyed activities, fatigue and low energy, changes in appetite (increased or decreased), sleep disturbances, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness or guilt, withdrawal from social activities  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Maintain a daily routine — set small achievable goals each day  
2. Exercise regularly — even a 20-minute walk elevates mood (releases endorphins)  
3. Ensure adequate sleep — aim for 7–9 hours with consistent schedule  
4. Eat a balanced diet — omega-3 rich foods (fish, walnuts), fruits, and vegetables support brain health  
5. Stay socially connected — resist the urge to isolate  
6. Practice gratitude journaling — write 3 things you are grateful for each day  
7. Spend time outdoors in natural sunlight (helps regulate serotonin)  
8. Limit social media consumption if it worsens mood  

**When to seek help:** symptoms persist >2 weeks, thoughts of self-harm or suicide (call helpline immediately), inability to perform daily tasks, significant weight changes, dependence on substances  

**DO NOT:** ignore persistent symptoms, stop prescribed medication without consulting a doctor, use alcohol or drugs to cope, blame yourself for how you feel  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Panic Attacks
**Symptoms:** sudden intense fear or discomfort peaking within minutes, rapid heartbeat (palpitations), sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, chest tightness, nausea, dizziness, numbness or tingling, feeling of unreality (derealization), fear of losing control or dying  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Grounding technique (5-4-3-2-1): Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste  
2. Slow breathing: breathe in through the nose for 4 seconds, out through the mouth for 6 seconds  
3. Remind yourself: "This is a panic attack. It is temporary. It is not dangerous. It will pass."  
4. Focus on a physical object — describe it in detail to redirect attention  
5. After the episode: rest, drink water, talk to someone you trust  
6. Regular exercise and mindfulness practice can reduce frequency of attacks  

**When to seek help:** panic attacks happen frequently (>once per week), symptoms mimic heart attack (seek emergency evaluation first time), avoidance of places/situations due to fear of attacks (agoraphobia), significant impact on quality of life  

**DO NOT:** hyperventilate (breathe into cupped hands if struggling), avoid all triggers indefinitely (graduated exposure helps), consume stimulants (caffeine, energy drinks)  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE
