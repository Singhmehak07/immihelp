## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Menstrual Cramps (Dysmenorrhea)
**Symptoms:** cramping pain in the lower abdomen, dull aching pain that may radiate to lower back and thighs, pain starting 1–2 days before period, nausea, headache, fatigue, loose stools  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Take Ibuprofen 400mg with food every 8 hours — start at first sign of pain or 1 day before expected period (NSAIDs are most effective for menstrual pain)  
2. Apply a heating pad or hot water bottle to the lower abdomen  
3. Take a warm bath to relax muscles  
4. Gentle exercise: walking, yoga, or light stretching can reduce cramps  
5. Try herbal teas: ginger tea, chamomile tea, or cinnamon tea  
6. Massage the lower abdomen in circular motions  
7. Ensure adequate magnesium intake (dark chocolate, nuts, bananas)  

**When to seek help:** severe pain not relieved by OTC painkillers, very heavy bleeding (soaking through a pad/tampon every hour for >2 hours), large blood clots, pain outside of menstrual period, sudden change in pattern of cramps  

**DO NOT:** rely on aspirin for menstrual pain (less effective and may increase bleeding), skip meals, use excessive caffeine (can worsen cramps)  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Vaginal Yeast Infection (Candidiasis)
**Symptoms:** intense itching and irritation of the vagina and vulva, thick white odorless cottage-cheese-like discharge, burning during urination, redness and swelling of the vulva, soreness, rash  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Use an OTC antifungal treatment: Clotrimazole vaginal cream (1%) for 7 days or Miconazole vaginal suppository  
2. Maintain proper hygiene — clean with warm water, avoid douching  
3. Wear loose, breathable cotton underwear  
4. Change out of wet swimwear or sweaty clothes promptly  
5. Eat probiotic-rich foods: yogurt with live cultures, kefir  
6. Avoid scented feminine products (soaps, sprays, pads)  
7. Reduce sugar intake — high blood sugar promotes yeast growth  

**When to seek help:** first-time infection (confirm diagnosis), symptoms do not improve within 3 days, symptoms recur within 2 months (>4 infections/year), pregnancy, foul-smelling discharge (may indicate bacterial vaginosis), fever or pelvic pain  

**DO NOT:** douche (disrupts natural vaginal flora), use scented products in the vaginal area, have unprotected intercourse during active infection, self-diagnose repeatedly — seek confirmation  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Migraine Headache
**Symptoms:** intense throbbing or pulsing pain usually on one side of the head, nausea and vomiting, sensitivity to light (photophobia) and sound (phonophobia), visual disturbances (aura) — flashing lights, zigzag lines, blind spots, pain lasting 4–72 hours  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Rest in a quiet, dark room at the onset of symptoms  
2. Apply a cold pack or ice wrapped in a cloth to the forehead or temples  
3. Take OTC pain relief early: Ibuprofen 400mg or combination of Paracetamol + Aspirin + Caffeine (e.g., Excedrin) — most effective when taken at first sign  
4. Stay hydrated — dehydration is a common trigger  
5. Try peppermint oil on the temples  
6. Practice relaxation techniques: deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation  
7. Identify and avoid personal triggers: keep a migraine diary tracking food, sleep, stress, weather, hormones  
8. Common triggers: red wine, aged cheese, chocolate, MSG, bright lights, strong smells, poor sleep, skipping meals  

**When to seek help:** worst headache of your life (thunderclap headache — emergency), headache with fever and stiff neck, headache after head injury, new headache pattern after age 50, aura lasting >1 hour, migraines >4 days per month (may need preventive therapy)  

**DO NOT:** overuse painkillers (>10 days/month — causes medication overuse headache), skip meals, consume excessive caffeine (both trigger and withdrawal can cause migraines), ignore sudden severe headache with different character  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE
