## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Acid Reflux / Heartburn (GERD)
**Symptoms:** burning sensation in chest (heartburn), acid taste in the back of the mouth, difficulty swallowing, regurgitation of food or sour liquid, chest pain when lying down, chronic cough especially at night  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Take OTC antacids (aluminum/magnesium hydroxide) for quick relief  
2. Use H2 blockers (Famotidine 20mg) or Omeprazole 20mg once daily for persistent symptoms  
3. Eat smaller, more frequent meals instead of large meals  
4. Avoid trigger foods: spicy, citrus, tomatoes, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, fatty/fried foods  
5. Do not eat within 2–3 hours of lying down  
6. Elevate the head of the bed by 15–20 cm (use bed risers, not just pillows)  
7. Maintain a healthy weight — excess abdominal fat increases reflux  

**When to seek help:** difficulty swallowing worsens, unintentional weight loss, vomiting blood or dark stools (GI bleeding), chest pain that could be cardiac, symptoms persist despite 2 weeks of OTC PPIs  

**DO NOT:** lie down immediately after eating, wear tight belts or clothing around the waist, smoke (weakens the lower esophageal sphincter)  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Food Poisoning (Acute Gastroenteritis)
**Symptoms:** nausea and vomiting, watery diarrhea, abdominal cramps, fever, headache, muscle aches, symptoms usually begin 2–6 hours after eating contaminated food  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Rest the stomach — avoid solid food for a few hours after vomiting  
2. Sip small amounts of clear fluids: water, ORS, clear broth, ginger ale  
3. Reintroduce bland foods gradually: BRAT diet (Bananas, Rice, Applesauce, Toast)  
4. Take ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution) to replace lost electrolytes  
5. Ginger tea or ginger chews can help reduce nausea  
6. Once improving, avoid dairy, fatty, spicy, and sugary foods for 48 hours  

**When to seek help:** bloody vomit or stool, unable to keep any fluids down for >12 hours, high fever >38.5°C, signs of severe dehydration (dark urine, dizziness, dry mouth), symptoms last >3 days, symptoms in infants, elderly, or immunocompromised  

**DO NOT:** take anti-diarrheal medications (Loperamide) if fever or blood in stool is present, force eating, use antibiotics without prescription  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Hemorrhoids (Piles)
**Symptoms:** itching or irritation around the anus, pain or discomfort, swelling around anus, painless bleeding during bowel movements (bright red blood on tissue), a lump near the anus that may be sensitive  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Take warm sitz baths for 15–20 minutes, 2–3 times daily  
2. Apply witch hazel pads or OTC hemorrhoid cream (with hydrocortisone) for itching and swelling  
3. Eat high-fiber foods: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes (aim for 25–30g fiber/day)  
4. Drink at least 2 liters of water daily to soften stools  
5. Use a stool softener (Docusate Sodium) if needed  
6. Avoid straining — do not sit on the toilet for extended periods  
7. Use moist toilet wipes instead of dry toilet paper  

**When to seek help:** significant rectal bleeding, large external hemorrhoid that does not improve, severe pain (may indicate thrombosed hemorrhoid), prolapsed hemorrhoid that cannot be pushed back, anemia symptoms (fatigue, pale skin)  

**DO NOT:** strain during bowel movements, sit on the toilet for more than 5–10 minutes, use dry toilet paper aggressively, lift very heavy weights  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE
