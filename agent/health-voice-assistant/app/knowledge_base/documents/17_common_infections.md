## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Influenza (Seasonal Flu)
**Symptoms:** sudden high fever (38–40°C), severe body aches and muscle pain, headache, extreme fatigue, dry cough, sore throat, runny or stuffy nose, chills and sweating  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Rest in bed — the body needs energy to fight the virus  
2. Drink plenty of fluids: water, herbal tea, warm broth, ORS if needed  
3. Take Paracetamol 500mg every 6 hours for fever and pain  
4. Use saline nasal spray for congestion  
5. Gargle warm salt water for sore throat  
6. Use a humidifier to ease breathing  
7. Eat light nutritious meals even if appetite is low (soup, toast, fruit)  
8. Isolate from others to prevent spreading — flu is highly contagious for 5–7 days  

**When to seek help:** difficulty breathing, chest pain, persistent vomiting, confusion, high-risk groups (elderly, infants, pregnant, immunocompromised), symptoms worsen after initial improvement (secondary infection)  

**DO NOT:** give aspirin to children or teenagers (risk of Reye's syndrome), go to work or school while symptomatic, take antibiotics (flu is viral — antibiotics do not help)  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Chickenpox (Varicella)
**Symptoms:** itchy blister-like rash starting on trunk and spreading, fever, fatigue, headache, loss of appetite, rash progresses from spots to blisters to crusts over 5–7 days  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Apply calamine lotion to soothe itching  
2. Take lukewarm baths with colloidal oatmeal or baking soda (1/2 cup per bath)  
3. Keep nails short and consider cotton gloves for children at night to prevent scratching  
4. Take Paracetamol for fever (NOT aspirin or ibuprofen)  
5. Use antihistamines (Cetirizine or Diphenhydramine) for severe itching  
6. Wear loose, soft cotton clothing  
7. Isolate the patient until all blisters have crusted over (usually 5–7 days)  

**When to seek help:** rash spreads to the eyes, high fever >39°C persisting >4 days, signs of skin infection (increased redness, warmth, pus), difficulty breathing, severe headache with stiff neck, immunocompromised patients, newborns or pregnant women  

**DO NOT:** give aspirin or ibuprofen to children with chickenpox (risk of Reye's syndrome and skin complications), scratch blisters (can cause scarring and infection), expose pregnant women or immunocompromised people  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: HIGH
### Condition: Typhoid Fever
**Symptoms:** sustained high fever (39–40°C) rising in a stepladder pattern, headache, abdominal pain, constipation or diarrhea, rose-colored spots on chest, weakness and fatigue, loss of appetite  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Keep the patient comfortable and resting in bed  
2. Maintain hydration — small frequent sips of water, ORS, coconut water, clear soups  
3. Use Paracetamol for fever management (avoid NSAIDs — risk of GI bleeding)  
4. Provide easily digestible food: khichdi, porridge, boiled potatoes, bananas  
5. Monitor temperature regularly  
6. Practice strict hand hygiene and food safety  

**When to seek help:** immediately if typhoid is suspected (requires antibiotic treatment), severe abdominal pain (risk of perforation), confusion, persistent high fever not responding to Paracetamol, rash appears  

**DO NOT:** self-treat with antibiotics, eat raw or undercooked food, delay seeking medical diagnosis, give anti-diarrheal drugs without medical guidance  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Common Cold (Viral Upper Respiratory Infection)
**Symptoms:** runny or stuffy nose, sneezing, mild sore throat, mild cough, slight body aches, low-grade fever, watery eyes, general malaise  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Rest and sleep — allow the immune system to fight the virus  
2. Drink warm fluids: herbal tea with honey and lemon, warm water, broth  
3. Use saline nasal drops or spray to relieve congestion  
4. Take Paracetamol for headache and mild fever  
5. Gargle warm salt water for sore throat  
6. Use a humidifier or steam inhalation to ease congestion  
7. Take Vitamin C (500–1000mg/day) and Zinc lozenges — may reduce duration  
8. Wash hands frequently and cover coughs/sneezes  

**When to seek help:** symptoms persist >10 days, high fever >39°C, difficulty breathing, severe sinus pain, symptoms worsen after initial improvement  

**DO NOT:** take antibiotics (colds are viral), give cough and cold medicines to children under 6, use decongestant sprays >3 days  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE
