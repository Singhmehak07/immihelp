## RISK_LEVEL: HIGH
### Condition: Asthma Flare-up (Mild to Moderate)
**Symptoms:** wheezing (whistling sound when breathing), shortness of breath, chest tightness, coughing (especially at night or early morning), difficulty speaking in full sentences, increased breathing rate  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Use rescue inhaler (Salbutamol/Albuterol) — 2–4 puffs via spacer, wait 1 minute between puffs  
2. Sit upright — do not lie down during an attack  
3. Stay calm and take slow, steady breaths  
4. Move away from triggers (dust, smoke, pollen, cold air, exercise)  
5. If symptoms improve with rescue inhaler, rest and monitor closely  
6. Keep a peak flow meter and track readings if available  
7. Ensure preventer inhaler (if prescribed) is being used regularly — not just during attacks  

**When to seek help:** no improvement after 10–15 minutes despite rescue inhaler, unable to speak in full sentences, lips or fingernails turning blue, peak flow <50% of personal best, severe breathing difficulty, using accessory muscles to breathe  

**DO NOT:** ignore worsening symptoms, lie flat during an attack, use a nebulizer without medical guidance for severe attacks, skip preventer medications when feeling well  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Acute Bronchitis
**Symptoms:** persistent cough (may produce clear, white, yellow, or green mucus), chest discomfort, fatigue, mild body aches, mild headache, sore throat, slight fever and chills, shortness of breath  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Rest and get plenty of sleep  
2. Drink warm fluids: herbal tea with honey, warm water with lemon, broth  
3. Use a humidifier or steam inhalation to loosen mucus  
4. Take Paracetamol or Ibuprofen for fever and aches  
5. Use honey (1–2 teaspoons) as a natural cough suppressant — as effective as some OTC cough medicines  
6. Try OTC expectorant (Guaifenesin) to help thin and expel mucus  
7. Avoid irritants: cigarette smoke, dust, strong fumes  
8. Gargle warm salt water for sore throat  

**When to seek help:** cough persists >3 weeks, coughing up blood, high fever >38.5°C lasting >3 days, severe shortness of breath, symptoms worsen significantly, underlying conditions (COPD, heart disease)  

**DO NOT:** take antibiotics without prescription (most bronchitis is viral), smoke or be exposed to secondhand smoke, suppress a productive cough (body needs to clear mucus)  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE
