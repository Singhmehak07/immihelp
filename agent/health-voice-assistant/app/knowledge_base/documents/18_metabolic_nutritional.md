## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Mild to Moderate Dehydration
**Symptoms:** increased thirst, dry mouth and lips, dark yellow urine, reduced urine output, fatigue, dizziness when standing, dry skin, headache  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Drink ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution): mix 6 teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt in 1 liter of clean water  
2. Sip fluids frequently rather than drinking large amounts at once  
3. If due to vomiting: wait 15–20 minutes after vomiting, then sip small amounts  
4. Coconut water is an excellent natural rehydration drink  
5. Avoid caffeinated and alcoholic beverages (they worsen dehydration)  
6. Eat water-rich fruits: watermelon, cucumber, oranges  
7. For children: offer ORS with a spoon or syringe every few minutes  

**When to seek help:** no urine output for >8 hours (adults) or >6 hours (children), sunken eyes, rapid heartbeat, confusion or lethargy, inability to keep any fluids down, bloody diarrhea, infants with dry diapers for >6 hours  

**DO NOT:** give plain water alone for rehydration (lacks electrolytes), give sugary drinks like soda as primary rehydration, wait until severely dehydrated to start fluids  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Iron Deficiency Anemia (Mild)
**Symptoms:** fatigue and weakness, pale skin, shortness of breath on exertion, dizziness, cold hands and feet, brittle nails, headache, unusual cravings (ice, dirt — pica), fast heartbeat  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Eat iron-rich foods daily: red meat, liver, spinach, lentils, chickpeas, fortified cereals, tofu  
2. Pair iron-rich foods with Vitamin C sources (orange juice, lemon, bell peppers) to enhance absorption  
3. Cook in cast iron pots and pans — increases iron content of food  
4. Take OTC iron supplement (Ferrous Sulfate 325mg) if advised — take on an empty stomach or with Vitamin C  
5. Drink iron-absorption-enhancing beverages: orange juice with meals  
6. Avoid tea, coffee, and calcium-rich foods with iron-rich meals (they inhibit absorption)  

**When to seek help:** severe fatigue affecting daily life, chest pain, rapid or irregular heartbeat, shortness of breath at rest, cravings for non-food items, heavy menstrual periods  

**DO NOT:** take iron supplements with tea, coffee, or dairy products, exceed recommended iron supplement dose (can cause constipation and toxicity), ignore symptoms assuming it is just tiredness  

**Escalation_Required:** TRUE

## RISK_LEVEL: LOW
### Condition: Vitamin D Deficiency
**Symptoms:** fatigue, bone pain, muscle weakness and aches, frequent infections, low mood or depression, slow wound healing, hair loss, bone loss (osteopenia)  

**Home Care Advice:**
1. Get safe sun exposure: 15–20 minutes of midday sunlight on arms and face, 3–4 times per week  
2. Eat Vitamin D-rich foods: fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines), egg yolks, fortified milk, mushrooms exposed to sunlight  
3. Take OTC Vitamin D3 supplement: 1000–2000 IU daily (or as advised by healthcare provider)  
4. Take Vitamin D with a meal containing fat for better absorption  
5. Ensure adequate calcium intake alongside Vitamin D (they work together for bone health)  

**When to seek help:** severe bone pain, frequent fractures, extreme fatigue, muscle cramps or spasms, symptoms of osteoporosis, suspected Vitamin D level <20 ng/mL  

**DO NOT:** take excessive Vitamin D supplements (toxicity risk — hypercalcemia), rely solely on supplements without dietary and lifestyle changes, avoid all sun exposure  

**Escalation_Required:** FALSE
