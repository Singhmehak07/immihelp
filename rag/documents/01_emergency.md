# Difficulty breathing / Cannot breathe

## SYMPTOMS
- Gasping for air or struggling to breathe
- Wheezing or noisy breathing
- Lips, face, or fingernails turning blue or pale
- Inability to speak in full sentences or at all
- Severe chest tightness

## HOME CARE ADVICE
1. Call emergency services immediately.
2. Help the person sit down and lean slightly forward, resting their hands on their knees to make breathing easier.
3. If they have a prescribed asthma inhaler, help them find and use it.
4. Loosen any tight clothing around their neck, chest, and waist.
5. Offer constant reassurance to keep them calm, as panic can worsen breathing difficulties.

## DO NOT
- Do not force the person to lie down.
- Do not give them anything to eat or drink.
- Do not leave them alone.

## WHEN TO SEEK HELP
IMMEDIATELY — this is a life-threatening emergency

## KEYWORDS
asthma, breathing difficulty, shortness of breath, cyanosis, wheezing, respiratory distress

## SYNONYMS
- I can't breathe
- My chest is tight
- Gasping for air
- Wheezing
- Fighting for breath

# Chest pain / Heart attack

## SYMPTOMS
- Heavy pressure, tightness, or squeezing pain in the center of the chest
- Pain spreading to the arms, shoulders, neck, jaw, or back
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweat, pale skin, or nausea
- Extreme weakness or dizziness

## HOME CARE ADVICE
1. Call emergency services immediately.
2. Have the person sit down quietly, rest, and try to keep calm. Keep them completely still.
3. Loosen tight clothing around their neck, chest, and waist.
4. If they have prescribed chest pain medication (like nitroglycerin), help them take it.
5. If they are alert and not allergic, encourage them to chew and swallow a regular adult aspirin (300mg). Be prepared to start CPR if they become unresponsive.

## DO NOT
- Do not let the person drive themselves to the hospital.
- Do not dismiss the pain as indigestion or wait to see if it goes away.
- Do not let them walk around or exert themselves.

## WHEN TO SEEK HELP
IMMEDIATELY — this is a life-threatening emergency

## KEYWORDS
chest pain, heart attack, myocardial infarction, angina, squeezing chest

## SYNONYMS
- Elephant sitting on my chest
- Heart pain
- Chest squeezing
- Crushing chest pain

# Unconscious / Unresponsive

## SYMPTOMS
- No response when you gently shake them or shout their name
- No normal breathing or only occasional gasping
- Limp body

## HOME CARE ADVICE
1. Call emergency services immediately and ask for an AED (Automated External Defibrillator) if one is nearby.
2. Roll the person onto their back on a firm, flat surface.
3. Check for breathing. If they are not breathing or only gasping, begin CPR immediately. Push hard and fast in the center of the chest (at least 2 inches deep, 100-120 compressions per minute).
4. If an AED arrives, turn it on and follow the voice prompts immediately.
5. If the person is breathing perfectly normally but unresponsive, carefully roll them onto their side (recovery position) to keep their airway open.

## DO NOT
- Do not put a pillow under their head.
- Do not put anything in their mouth or try to give them fluids.
- Do not stop CPR once you start, unless they wake up or medical professionals take over.

## WHEN TO SEEK HELP
IMMEDIATELY — this is a life-threatening emergency

## KEYWORDS
unconscious, unresponsive, passing out, fainted, knocked out, CPR, no pulse

## SYNONYMS
- Passed out
- Won't wake up
- Fainted and not responding
- Blacked out
- Dropped down

# Seizure / Convulsion

## SYMPTOMS
- Sudden, uncontrollable violent shaking or jerking of the body
- Loss of consciousness or awareness
- Stiffening of the body
- Foaming at the mouth or drooling
- Temporary confusion afterward

## HOME CARE ADVICE
1. Note the time the seizure starts and clear the area of hard, sharp, or dangerous objects to prevent injury.
2. Carefully place something soft and flat, like a folded jacket, under their head.
3. Loosen any tight clothing around their neck and remove their glasses if they wear them.
4. Do not interfere with their movements—let the seizure run its course.
5. Once the shaking stops, gently roll the person onto their side (recovery position) to help them breathe and allow fluids to drain from their mouth. Call emergency services.

## DO NOT
- Do not hold the person down or try to stop the shaking.
- Do not put anything in their mouth (they cannot swallow their tongue).
- Do not offer food or water until they are fully alert and aware.

## WHEN TO SEEK HELP
IMMEDIATELY — this is a life-threatening emergency

## KEYWORDS
seizure, convulsion, epilepsy, fit, shaking, spasms, postictal

## SYNONYMS
- Having a fit
- Jerking uncontrollably
- Shaking spell
- Spasms

# Severe bleeding (uncontrolled)

## SYMPTOMS
- Blood spurting or pumping out of a wound
- Blood quickly soaking through clothing or bandages
- Large pools of blood forming on the ground
- Amputation or partial amputation
- The person becomes pale, cold, or dizzy

## HOME CARE ADVICE
1. Call emergency services immediately.
2. Put on medical gloves if available. Apply firm, constant, direct pressure on the wound using a clean cloth, sterile dressing, or your hands.
3. Keep pushing down heavily. If blood soaks through, do not remove the cloth; just add more layers on top and press harder.
4. If the bleeding is from an arm or leg and pressure does not stop it, and you are trained, apply a tourniquet 2-3 inches above the wound (not on a joint) and tighten until the bleeding stops. Note the time it was applied.
5. Keep the person lying down, elevate their legs if possible, and cover them with a blanket to prevent shock.

## DO NOT
- Do not remove the initial dressing once applied, as this will pull off clotting blood.
- Do not wash a wound that is bleeding heavily.
- Do not remove any object that is deeply stuck in the wound (apply pressure around it instead).

## WHEN TO SEEK HELP
IMMEDIATELY — this is a life-threatening emergency

## KEYWORDS
severe bleeding, hemorrhage, uncontrolled bleeding, deep cut, amputation, gushing blood

## SYNONYMS
- Bleeding won't stop
- Squirt of blood
- Lost a lot of blood
- Cut wide open
- Sliced open

# Choking

## SYMPTOMS
- Clutching the throat with hands (the universal sign of choking)
- Unable to speak, cough effectively, or breathe
- Skin, lips, and nail beds turning pale or blue
- Panicked, distressed expression
- High-pitched squeaking noises when trying to breathe

## HOME CARE ADVICE
1. Ask the person "Are you choking?" If they nod and cannot speak, tell someone to call emergency services immediately while you help them.
2. Stand behind them and give up to 5 strong back blows: hit them firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. If the object doesn't come out, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver): make a fist above their belly button, grab it with your other hand, and pull inward and upward quickly.
4. Alternate between 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the blockage comes out and they can breathe.
5. If the person becomes completely unresponsive, carefully lower them to the floor and start CPR (chest compressions). Check the mouth for the object before attempting rescue breaths.

## DO NOT
- Do not stick your fingers in their throat to feel for an object unless you can see it and easily sweep it out.
- Do not give abdominal thrusts to an infant under 1 year old (use back slaps and chest thrusts instead).
- Do not offer water or food to try to wash the blockage down.

## WHEN TO SEEK HELP
IMMEDIATELY — this is a life-threatening emergency

## KEYWORDS
choking, blocked airway, obstruction, Heimlich maneuver, can't swallow

## SYNONYMS
- Something is stuck in my throat
- Swallowed wrong
- Gagging
- Suffocating
